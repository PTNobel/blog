I recently have been reading more complexity theory then is probably healthy for me for no particular reason.
I have formed a bunch of evidenceless conjectures that I thought I would share in case I am ever proven right or wrong.

## Commonly-held beliefs

I believe $\mathsf{P} \neq \mathsf{NP}$.
It feels inutitively obvious that search and verification should be different, and in particular, it seems clear to me that mixed-integer programming should be harder than linear programming.
I find it much harder to reason about the geometry of lattices versus polyhedrons.

I believe $\mathsf{BPP} \neq \mathsf{BQP}$.
Ultimately, I think quantum computers are cool and probably worth something in a complexity theory sense.

I believe $\mathsf{P} = \mathsf{BPP}$.
PRNGs work so empirically well in RNLA, that I can't imagine there's some true separation between PRNGs and true randomness.

I I believe simplex is inherently superpolynomial in the worst-case.

I believe that there is a method to solve LPs whose arithmetic complexity is polynomial, regardless of the magnitude of the coefficients.


## Beliefs I haven't heard before

I think matrix multiplication is ultimately $O(n^2 \log^3 n)$.
I, frankly, have no idea how the current methods to find $\omega$ work.
That said, $n^2$ is particularly elegant for the exponent and I feel like a $3$ should be there somewhere because of the three indices, so I take the $\log$ to the third power.

## Heterodox beliefs

I think the polynomial hierarchy collapses at level 17.
I think if the hierarchy collapses, it does so at a prime number, because primes are cool.
Since Knuth thinks that it does collapses (albeit much earlier than me), I will conjecture it does as well.
I think 17 would be a fun prime for the collapse to occur.
